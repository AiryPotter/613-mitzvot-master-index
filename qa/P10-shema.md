# Q&A — Mitzvah 10: Reciting the Shema

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Guide # | 10 |
| Rambam # | Positive Commandment 10 (P10) |
| Type | Positive |
| Verse cited | Deuteronomy 6:7 |
| Mishneh Torah | *Hilchot Keriat Shema* (Laws of Reading the Shema) |
| Can it be observed today? | Yes. It does not depend on the Temple, the Land of Israel, a court, or the priesthood. |

---

### What is the commandment?
To recite the Shema twice every day, in the evening and in the morning. Maimonides defines it as "the command to recite the Shema evening and morning" (*Sefer HaMitzvot*, Positive 10).

### Where does it come from in the Torah?
Deuteronomy 6:7: *"and thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thy house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up"* (JPS 1917). Maimonides bases the commandment on the words "and shalt talk of them" (*ve-dibbarta bam*). The phrase "when thou liest down, and when thou risest up" sets the times: when people lie down (night) and when they get up (morning) (Mishneh Torah, Keriat Shema 1:1).

### What is the context of the verse?
It comes right after "Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one" (Deut. 6:4). Moses is teaching the people, as they prepare to enter the Land, to keep God's words always on their heart and lips. The same passage is the source of the commandments to study Torah (Mitzvah 11), wear tefillin (Mitzvot 12–13), and affix a mezuzah (Mitzvah 15).

### Is the Shema a Torah commandment or a rabbinic one?
Maimonides holds that it comes from the Torah itself. He cites Talmud Berakhot 21a for this. The blessings recited before and after it are rabbinic.

### What exactly is recited?
Three Torah passages, in this order (Mishneh Torah, Keriat Shema 1:2):
1. Deuteronomy 6:4–9. It declares God's unity and the love of God, which Maimonides calls the great principle on which everything depends.
2. Deuteronomy 11:13–21. It speaks of accepting all the commandments.
3. Numbers 15:37–41. It contains the commandment of tzitzit and the remembrance of the Exodus.

After the first verse, the line *Barukh shem kevod malkhuto le-olam va-ed* ("Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever") is said quietly. This line is not in the Torah; it is an ancient tradition (Keriat Shema 1:4).

### When is it said?
- **Evening:** from nightfall (when the stars come out) until midnight is the preferred time. After the fact, one has fulfilled the obligation if one says it before dawn (Keriat Shema 1:9; Mishnah Berakhot 1:1).
- **Morning:** ideally finishing at sunrise. After the fact, the obligation can still be fulfilled until the end of the third hour of the day (Keriat Shema 1:11; Mishnah Berakhot 1:2).

### Which blessings go with it?
In the morning, two blessings before and one after. In the evening, two before and two after (Mishnah Berakhot 1:4; Keriat Shema 1:5).

### Who is obligated?
Adult Jewish men. Women are exempt because it is a commandment with a fixed time (Mishnah Berakhot 3:3; Keriat Shema 4:1). Children are taught to say it as training. Today many women also recite it.

### How is it said?
- **Intention:** one must concentrate on the meaning of at least the first verse. Without that intention, one has not fulfilled the obligation (Keriat Shema 2:1).
- **Posture:** sitting, standing, walking, or lying on one's side are all allowed. One who is walking stops for the first verse (2:2–3).
- **Voice:** say it loudly enough to hear oneself, and pronounce the words carefully (2:8).
- **Covering the eyes:** many cover their eyes with the right hand during the first verse, following Rabbi Judah the Prince (Talmud Berakhot 13b).
- **Language:** it may be said in any language the person understands (Keriat Shema 2:10; Mishnah Sotah 7:1).

### Important disagreements
- **How much does the Torah require?** The Talmud debates whether the Torah obligation covers all three passages, only the first section, or only the first verse (Berakhot 13b).
- **How much intention is needed?** It is debated how much of the Shema requires focused intention. Maimonides requires it for the first verse only (2:1).
- **Is the third passage required at night?** The tzitzit passage is said at night even though tzitzit is a daytime commandment, because it mentions the Exodus (Mishnah Berakhot 1:5).

## The text of the Shema

Hebrew: *Tanach with Nikkud* (Public Domain). English: *The Holy Scriptures*, JPS 1917 (Public Domain). In reading, the divine name יְהוָה is pronounced *Adonai*; JPS translates it "the LORD."

### First passage — Deuteronomy 6:4–9
שְׁמַע יִשְׂרָאֵל יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ יְהוָה אֶחָד׃
*(quietly)* בָּרוּךְ שֵׁם כְּבוֹד מַלְכוּתוֹ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד׃
וְאָהַבְתָּ אֵת יְהוָה אֱלֹהֶיךָ בְּכָל־לְבָבְךָ וּבְכָל־נַפְשְׁךָ וּבְכָל־מְאֹדֶךָ׃ וְהָיוּ הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוְּךָ הַיּוֹם עַל־לְבָבֶךָ׃ וְשִׁנַּנְתָּם לְבָנֶיךָ וְדִבַּרְתָּ בָּם בְּשִׁבְתְּךָ בְּבֵיתֶךָ וּבְלֶכְתְּךָ בַדֶּרֶךְ וּבְשָׁכְבְּךָ וּבְקוּמֶךָ׃ וּקְשַׁרְתָּם לְאוֹת עַל־יָדֶךָ וְהָיוּ לְטֹטָפֹת בֵּין עֵינֶיךָ׃ וּכְתַבְתָּם עַל־מְזוּזֹת בֵּיתֶךָ וּבִשְׁעָרֶיךָ׃

Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one.
*(quietly)* Blessed be the name of His glorious kingdom forever and ever.
And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thy heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be upon thy heart; and thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thy house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up. And thou shalt bind them for a sign upon thy hand, and they shall be for frontlets between thine eyes. And thou shalt write them upon the door-posts of thy house, and upon thy gates.

### Second passage — Deuteronomy 11:13–21
וְהָיָה אִם־שָׁמֹעַ תִּשְׁמְעוּ אֶל־מִצְוֺתַי אֲשֶׁר אָנֹכִי מְצַוֶּה אֶתְכֶם הַיּוֹם לְאַהֲבָה אֶת־יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם וּלְעָבְדוֹ בְּכָל־לְבַבְכֶם וּבְכָל־נַפְשְׁכֶם׃ וְנָתַתִּי מְטַר־אַרְצְכֶם בְּעִתּוֹ יוֹרֶה וּמַלְקוֹשׁ וְאָסַפְתָּ דְגָנֶךָ וְתִירֹשְׁךָ וְיִצְהָרֶךָ׃ וְנָתַתִּי עֵשֶׂב בְּשָׂדְךָ לִבְהֶמְתֶּךָ וְאָכַלְתָּ וְשָׂבָעְתָּ׃ הִשָּׁמְרוּ לָכֶם פֶּן יִפְתֶּה לְבַבְכֶם וְסַרְתֶּם וַעֲבַדְתֶּם אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים וְהִשְׁתַּחֲוִיתֶם לָהֶם׃ וְחָרָה אַף־יְהוָה בָּכֶם וְעָצַר אֶת־הַשָּׁמַיִם וְלֹא־יִהְיֶה מָטָר וְהָאֲדָמָה לֹא תִתֵּן אֶת־יְבוּלָהּ וַאֲבַדְתֶּם מְהֵרָה מֵעַל הָאָרֶץ הַטֹּבָה אֲשֶׁר יְהוָה נֹתֵן לָכֶם׃ וְשַׂמְתֶּם אֶת־דְּבָרַי אֵלֶּה עַל־לְבַבְכֶם וְעַל־נַפְשְׁכֶם וּקְשַׁרְתֶּם אֹתָם לְאוֹת עַל־יֶדְכֶם וְהָיוּ לְטוֹטָפֹת בֵּין עֵינֵיכֶם׃ וְלִמַּדְתֶּם אֹתָם אֶת־בְּנֵיכֶם לְדַבֵּר בָּם בְּשִׁבְתְּךָ בְּבֵיתֶךָ וּבְלֶכְתְּךָ בַדֶּרֶךְ וּבְשָׁכְבְּךָ וּבְקוּמֶךָ׃ וּכְתַבְתָּם עַל־מְזוּזוֹת בֵּיתֶךָ וּבִשְׁעָרֶיךָ׃ לְמַעַן יִרְבּוּ יְמֵיכֶם וִימֵי בְנֵיכֶם עַל הָאֲדָמָה אֲשֶׁר נִשְׁבַּע יְהוָה לַאֲבֹתֵיכֶם לָתֵת לָהֶם כִּימֵי הַשָּׁמַיִם עַל־הָאָרֶץ׃

And it shall come to pass, if ye shall hearken diligently unto My commandments which I command you this day, to love the LORD your God, and to serve Him with all your heart and with all your soul, that I will give the rain of your land in its season, the former rain and the latter rain, that thou mayest gather in thy corn, and thy wine, and thine oil. And I will give grass in thy fields for thy cattle, and thou shalt eat and be satisfied. Take heed to yourselves, lest your heart be deceived, and ye turn aside, and serve other gods, and worship them; and the anger of the LORD be kindled against you, and He shut up the heaven, so that there shall be no rain, and the ground shall not yield her fruit; and ye perish quickly from off the good land which the LORD giveth you. Therefore shall ye lay up these My words in your heart and in your soul; and ye shall bind them for a sign upon your hand, and they shall be for frontlets between your eyes. And ye shall teach them your children, talking of them, when thou sittest in thy house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up. And thou shalt write them upon the door-posts of thy house, and upon thy gates; that your days may be multiplied, and the days of your children, upon the land which the LORD swore unto your fathers to give them, as the days of the heavens above the earth.

### Third passage — Numbers 15:37–41
וַיֹּאמֶר יְהוָה אֶל־מֹשֶׁה לֵּאמֹר׃ דַּבֵּר אֶל־בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל וְאָמַרְתָּ אֲלֵהֶם וְעָשׂוּ לָהֶם צִיצִת עַל־כַּנְפֵי בִגְדֵיהֶם לְדֹרֹתָם וְנָתְנוּ עַל־צִיצִת הַכָּנָף פְּתִיל תְּכֵלֶת׃ וְהָיָה לָכֶם לְצִיצִת וּרְאִיתֶם אֹתוֹ וּזְכַרְתֶּם אֶת־כָּל־מִצְוֺת יְהוָה וַעֲשִׂיתֶם אֹתָם וְלֹא־תָתֻרוּ אַחֲרֵי לְבַבְכֶם וְאַחֲרֵי עֵינֵיכֶם אֲשֶׁר־אַתֶּם זֹנִים אַחֲרֵיהֶם׃ לְמַעַן תִּזְכְּרוּ וַעֲשִׂיתֶם אֶת־כָּל־מִצְוֺתָי וִהְיִיתֶם קְדֹשִׁים לֵאלֹהֵיכֶם׃ אֲנִי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם אֲשֶׁר הוֹצֵאתִי אֶתְכֶם מֵאֶרֶץ מִצְרַיִם לִהְיוֹת לָכֶם לֵאלֹהִים אֲנִי יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם׃

And the LORD spoke unto Moses, saying: 'Speak unto the children of Israel, and bid them that they make them throughout their generations fringes in the corners of their garments, and that they put with the fringe of each corner a thread of blue. And it shall be unto you for a fringe, that ye may look upon it, and remember all the commandments of the LORD, and do them; and that ye go not about after your own heart and your own eyes, after which ye use to go astray; that ye may remember and do all My commandments, and be holy unto your God. I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God: I am the LORD your God.'
